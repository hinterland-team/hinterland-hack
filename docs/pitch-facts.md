## 1. Wayfinding study (US, 2025)

**Source:** Saman Jamshidi, Seyedehnastaran Hashemi, Dieu-My T. Tran. "Costs and Effects of Ineffective Wayfinding in US Hospitals: A Survey of Hospital Staff." HERD: Health Environments Research & Design Journal, Volume 18, Issue 2. First published online 13 Feb 2025, issue April 2025. DOI 10.1177/19375867251317240. Free access on the publisher site.
- Full text: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/19375867251317240
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39943920/
- Note: Sage lists pages 259-275, PubMed lists 463-479. Cite by DOI.

### 1a. "301 hospital staff in the US, 2025"
- Verdict: **confirmed.**
- Quote (abstract): "This descriptive cross-sectional study used an online questionnaire to gather data from hospital staff in the United States." ... "Data from 301 questionnaires were analyzed using descriptive and nonparametric statistics."

### 1b. "31.8 minutes per week" and 1c. "6.9 minutes per interaction"
- Verdict: **confirmed.**
- Correct figures: mean 31.82 min per week, median 15 min per week. Mean 6.90 min per instance. 4.09 instances per week.
- Quote (Results): "On average, staff members helped people find their destinations 4.09 times per week (SD = 4.62). The instances of assistance took 6.90 min on average (SD = 11.61). Consequently, each staff member spent about half an hour per week (M = 31.82, SD = 75.25) on helping other people find their way (95% CI [23.29, 40.36]), with a median of 15 min per week (Table 3 and Figure 1)."
- Caveat: the spread is large (SD 75 min) and the median is half the mean. Self-reported, US only. Safer to say "about 30 minutes per week on average".

### 1d. "Nearly 44% experienced rude or hostile behaviour from lost visitors"
- Verdict: **differs in wording.** The number is right. The paper says "incivility", not "rude or hostile". It is from "people (staff, patients, and visitors)", not only lost visitors. It counts the whole career, not one year.
- Quote (Results): "Nearly 44% of the participants (n = 131) had experienced incivility from people (staff, patients, and visitors) who had become frustrated due to their inability to find their way around the hospital during their careers."
- Quote (abstract): "Nearly 44% of the participants experienced incivility from users who had become frustrated by wayfinding problems."

### Other useful lines from the same paper
- "The most common approach, used by 36% of the staff, was to escort individuals to a point from which they could independently reach their final destinations. A slightly smaller proportion (29%) chose to accompany individuals directly to their final destinations. Approximately a quarter of the participants (27%) preferred to give verbal directions."
- "Accordingly, we estimate that in US hospitals, about 98,058,720 nurse hours are spent annually on wayfinding assistance. This figure is equivalent to 51,072 full-time positions (or 25,536 full-time positions if using the median time of 15 min per week)."
- Older figure, quoted inside this paper (we did not read the 1990 original): "A 1990 study estimated that 4,500 staff hours per year were spent on assisting people with wayfinding in a 604-bed tertiary hospital (Zimring, 1990), a figure that translates to about 7.75 h per bed annually."

## 3. EDPB Guidelines 3/2019 on processing of personal data through video devices

**Source:** European Data Protection Board, "Guidelines 3/2019 on processing of personal data through video devices", Version 2.0, adopted on 29 January 2020.
- Page: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-32019-on-processing-of-personal-data-through-video-devices_en
- PDF: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf

The guidelines are written for video surveillance. A robot that uses its camera live to see if someone is close behind is not classic surveillance, but the same GDPR principles apply. What matters for us:

**a) GDPR only stops applying when nobody can be identified.** A live camera image of a person in a corridor usually shows an identifiable person. So "no recording" does not mean "GDPR does not apply".
- Para 8: "However, the Regulation does not apply to processing of data that has no reference to a person, e.g. if an individual cannot be identified, directly or indirectly."

**b) Live-only is still processing, but after the moment nothing is held.**
- Para 92: "For video surveillance this means that if no data is stored or transferred in any way then once the real-time monitoring moment has passed the controller could only give the information that no personal data is any longer being processed (besides the general information obligations under Article 13, see section 7 – Transparency and information obligations)."

**c) People must be told a camera is in use (transparency).** Our on-screen privacy line helps. A real deployment also needs a sign / information notice.
- Para 110: "It has long been inherent in European data protection law that data subjects should be aware of the fact that video surveillance is in operation."

**d) Detecting a person without identifying them is not biometric (Art. 9) processing.** This supports "person detection only, no face recognition".
- Para 74: "The video footage of an individual cannot however in itself be considered as biometric data under Article 9, if it has not been specifically technically processed in order to contribute to the identification of an individual."
- Para 80: "However, when the purpose of the processing is for example to distinguish one category of people from another but not to uniquely identify anyone the processing does not fall under Article 9."

**e) Seeing a wheelchair is not health data by itself, but using video to monitor health is.** So Nova must not use the camera to judge a patient's condition.
- Para 62 (example): "Video footage showing a data subject wearing glasses or using a wheel chair are not per se considered to be special categories of personal data."
- Para 64 (example): "A hospital installing a video camera in order to monitor a patient's health condition would be considered as processing of special categories of personal data (Article 9)."

**f) Minimise data, prefer not recording, switch off what you don't need.**
- Para 24: "Personal data should be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (`data minimisation'), see Article 5 (1) (c)."
- Para 29: "In other situations, it might not be necessary to record the video material at all but more appropriate to use real-time monitoring instead."
- Para 129: "On the other hand, the selected solutions should not provide functions that are not necessary (e.g., unlimited movement of cameras, zoom capability, radio transmission, analysis and audio recordings). Functions provided, but not necessary, must be deactivated."

**g) Privacy by design before you start.**
- Para 126: "As stated in Article 25 GDPR, controllers need to implement appropriate data protection technical and organisational measures as soon as they plan for video surveillance – before they start the collection and processing of video footage."

**h) Storage (only relevant if anything were stored).** We store no images. Our journey data (ticket, room, times) is deleted 12 h after the end; this is our own rule, not an EDPB number.
- Para 121: "The longer the storage period set (especially when beyond 72 hours), the more argumentation for the legitimacy of the purpose and the necessity of storage has to be provided."

**Open point (not verified):** speech goes through the robot's built-in AI (OrionStar AgentOS). Where the audio is processed (on the robot or in a cloud) is not checked. Do not claim "nothing leaves the robot" for voice.

## 4. EU MDR 2017/745: is Nova Clinic a medical device?

**Source:** Regulation (EU) 2017/745 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 5 April 2017 on medical devices (OJ L 117, 5.5.2017).
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R0745

- Verdict: **confirmed** that classification follows the intended purpose.

**Definition, Article 2(1):** "'medical device' means any instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, implant, reagent, material or other article intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for one or more of the following specific medical purposes:
- diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease,
- diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of, or compensation for, an injury or disability,
- investigation, replacement or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological or pathological process or state,
- providing information by means of in vitro examination of specimens derived from the human body, including organ, blood and tissue donations,

and which does not achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means, in or on the human body, but which may be assisted in its function by such means."

**Intended purpose, Article 2(12):** "'intended purpose' means the use for which a device is intended according to the data supplied by the manufacturer on the label, in the instructions for use or in promotional or sales materials or statements and as specified by the manufacturer in the clinical evaluation;"

**Classification, Article 51(1):** "Devices shall be divided into classes I, IIa, IIb and III, taking into account the intended purpose of the devices and their inherent risks. Classification shall be carried out in accordance with Annex VIII."

**Annex VIII, 3.1:** "Application of the classification rules shall be governed by the intended purpose of the devices."

**Recital 19:** "It is necessary to clarify that software in its own right, when specifically intended by the manufacturer to be used for one or more of the medical purposes set out in the definition of a medical device, qualifies as a medical device, while software for general purposes, even when used in a healthcare setting, or software intended for life-style and well-being purposes is not a medical device."

**Why a pure navigation helper is likely not a medical device:**
- Its intended purpose is to guide people through a building. That is none of the medical purposes in Article 2(1).
- Recital 19 says general-purpose software is not a medical device just because it runs in a hospital. (A recital helps reading the law; it is not itself a rule.)
- Nova does not diagnose, triage, monitor health or choose medical destinations. Staff decide where a patient goes.

**Careful:**
- Article 2(1) includes "compensation for ... disability". So don't market Nova as an aid for disabled people. Market it as building navigation for everyone, with a slower walking pace as an option.
- The intended purpose includes "promotional or sales materials" (Art. 2(12)). The pitch itself counts. No medical claims.
- Wording for the pitch: "likely not a medical device; this needs a proper regulatory assessment before use in a hospital."
